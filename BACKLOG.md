## Open — high priority

**B-11 · Verify CORS on the four discovery endpoints**
PRD §8.6 assumes Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday send permissive CORS headers, so a Manifest V3 service worker can fetch them directly with `host_permissions` and no backend hop. This is tagged `[assumption]` and is unverified — the loop's sandbox refuses egress to all four hosts. One `curl -I` per host with an `Origin: chrome-extension://…` header from an ordinary network settles it. Blocks writing any adapter, because a refusal on a documented provider forces a proxy and changes the cost profile of the whole engine. **Dhwani can close this in about two minutes; the loop cannot close it at all.** Note from 2026-10-12: the same four requests now answer a second question. PRD §8.7 assumes the handle in a hosted board URL (`job-boards.greenhouse.io/{token}`, `jobs.lever.co/{site}`, `jobs.ashbyhq.com/{name}`, Workday's `{tenant}.{pod}/{site}`) is byte-identical to the handle the API path takes — which is what makes handle *harvesting* possible at all. No provider documents the equivalence, and the Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby docs all refused to open from the loop's sandbox. So each `curl` should use a handle lifted from a real board URL: if it returns that board's jobs, both assumptions close together. Note from 2026-10-13: PRD §9.5 adds a third thing worth reading off the same four responses — whether each provider's job objects carry a posting date at all, or only a mutable `updated_at`. Freshness caps at 3 of 5 points for any job whose only date is mutable, so the share of each board that caps is the difference between a working freshness term and a decorative one.

**B-15 · "Priority threshold" is load-bearing and has no number**
PRD §5.2 and §7.2 both gate on a job clearing the *priority threshold*, and §9 never defines one. It is the dial that decides how many jobs reach the brief at all, so an undefined threshold means §7's cap of 5 is doing all the work and the score is doing none. Two parts: pick a value on the 0–100 scale of §9.1, and decide whether it is fixed or adaptive (a fixed 70 produces empty days early on, when the watchlist is small; a rank-based "top 5 of today's qualified set" never does, but also never says no). Surfaced by B-02 on 2026-10-03. Asked of Dhwani on 2026-10-03, 2026-10-04, 2026-10-09, 2026-10-10, 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-12 — it is a judgment about what she wants to open at 8am, so the loop is holding rather than picking.

## Done

**B-01 · Fit score has no warm-connection term** — specified 2026-09-29 in PRD §9. Connection enters at 20 of 100, displacing 5 each from role match, experience match, company and narrative — the terms that predict fit but not conversation, and in the case of role/experience were double-counting one signal. The weights before that change, kept here because PRD §9.1 no longer carries its own history (R-03): role 30 / eligibility 20 / experience 15 / company 10 / narrative 10 / location 5 / authorization 5 / freshness 5, with no connection term at all. Tiers are scored by **what the person can do today** (C3 replied-or-will-refer 20 / C2 saved alum or contact, no reply 14 / C1 named but unsaved 8 / C0 nobody 0), never by an inferred closeness score, with C2 decaying to C1 after 21 silent days. Two CI-blocking invariants bound the term: connection re-ranks the qualified set but cannot admit a job below the role-fit floor, and C3 is worth exactly as much as company+narrative+location+freshness combined while staying smaller than role+experience. Explanation must name the person — if Hoshi cannot name them, it cannot award the points, which puts Principle 2 on the score itself. Weights and tier spacing tagged `[assumption]`: no public dataset resolves outcomes by connection strength, and B-04 has since established that one user's analytics cannot retire the tag either (PRD §10.4). The decay rule turned out to have a measurement consequence as well as a scoring one — see PRD §10.5, added 2026-10-09. §9.4 extended 2026-10-10: C1 must print where the person came from, not only who they are. **Invariant 2 corrected 2026-10-11:** it read *C3 exceeds the block* for twelve days, and 20 does not exceed 20 — the test is `==`, the guaranteed property is that a C3 job cannot be *outscored* on the four soft terms, and the tie is broken by §7.2's existing connection-tier tiebreak. Do not re-word it back, and do not bump a weight to make the old sentence true: that breaks the sum to 100.

**B-02 · Daily Brief leads with the wrong thing** — specified 2026-10-03 as PRD **§7**, and moved to the front of the document, ahead of the engines, because it is the only surface opened daily and §8–§10 exist to fill it. Three blocks in fixed order with hard caps: conversations (max 3), promoted jobs with their §9.4 score line (max 5), and one collapsed "also open" line. The caps are the substantive part — a Monster survey of 1,006 US job seekers found 48% apply without reading the full description and 32% spend under a minute on a posting ([HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/specialization/recruitment/application-overload-half-of-job-seekers-doomjobbing/578466)), so an unbounded ranked feed is the input that behaviour runs on. Promotion requires role fit ≥ 15 of 25 (§9.3 invariant 1) **and** either the priority threshold or tier ≥ C1; block 3 shows no score at all, because a number beside an unpromoted job invites the one-minute apply. Block 1 rows are generated from Hoshi's own stores and must name a person and a stored reason or they do not render. **The brief shows counts and never rates** — an interval cannot sit beside a CTA at 360px, and a daily surface invites exactly the comparison §10.3 rule 3 forbids. Promotion rules moved out of §8.1, which should not have been specifying a surface. Spawned B-15. Second tiebreak narrowed 2026-10-13 by B-07 — see below. No code written.

**B-03 · Discovery engine** — specified 2026-09-28 in PRD §8. Adapter interface, fail-closed result shape, per-provider field map onto `ExtractedJob`, dedup order reusing `identityKeys` with the page path winning over discovery, and the permission surface. Providers tiered: Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby are documented and unauthenticated; Workday is reverse-engineered and ships declaring `complete: false`. The design decision that makes it a Hoshi feature rather than a volume feature: **discovery is seeded by companies the user has a person at, not by keywords** — which is also the only shape available, since every documented provider endpoint is per-company with no cross-company search. LinkedIn and Indeed deliberately get no adapter. No code written; see B-11 before any is. **Extended 2026-10-12 with PRD §8.7**, which closes the hole §8.1 left open: a person gives a company *name* and every adapter takes a provider *handle*, with no documented name→handle lookup anywhere, so the watchlist rule did not actually produce a fetchable board. Handles come from three places only — harvested from the URL of a job page the user already opened (free: the §4 ATS fingerprint already parses that URL), pasted by the user as a careers URL, or nowhere, in which case the company stays on the list as **person-only**, seeding people and §9's connection term while contributing 0 jobs. Hoshi never probes candidate handles against a provider API to find out whether one exists (Principle 5). The hard case is the *embedded* board, where Greenhouse's own embed instructions put the token in a script tag on the company's careers page and leave it out of the URL entirely ([Greenhouse support](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/46365908766875-Embed-a-Greenhouse-job-board-on-your-career-site)) — Hoshi does not read that script (Principle 4, §8.4), so those boards resolve by paste or stay person-only. Do not re-propose a company→ATS directory lookup or token-probing as a fourth route. §8.4's date note replaced 2026-10-13 by a pointer to §9.5, which is now the only place the `updated_at` problem is specified.

**B-04 · No answer to "why did my response rate change?"** — specified 2026-10-02 in PRD §10. The finding that shaped it: **a single user cannot A/B test the application funnel at all.** Detecting a 5%→15% move in cold response rate needs 141 applications per arm, and a seeker reaching one interview per ~40 applications never gets there in a season. So the metric set leans to the warm path, where §1's base rate is an order of magnitude higher and therefore needs an order of magnitude fewer events, and the headline metric is **person-attached rate** — a proportion of the user's own behaviour rather than the market's, so it moves the same week. Metrics split into counts (exact, no interval) and rates (estimates, always with a 95% Wilson interval). Seven honesty rules replace "do not claim causality without enough data": no percentage under n=10, intervals at the same type size as the number, no comparative language unless two intervals are disjoint, no unsourceable external benchmark, and insufficient-data states that give the shortfall as a computed number. B-04's assigned job of falsifying §9.1's connection weight is **conceded as impossible at single-user scale** and recorded as such in §10.4, with two weaker substitutes. Spawned B-13. **Extended 2026-10-09** with an eighth honesty rule and §10.5: none of the tier or person-attached metrics may be computed from current state, because §9.2's tier changes after the event and the resulting bias runs in the direction that confirms §9.1. Spawned B-16. No code written.

**B-07 · Freshness is specified but unused** — resolved 2026-10-13 as PRD **§9.5**, by wiring rather than cutting. Freshness keeps its 5 of 100 and scores exactly one thing: **how full the applicant pool already is.** The band edges are measured, not chosen — DHI Group, linking ~80M applications to ~10M postings, reports 41% of applications arriving inside 48 hours and 56% inside 96, against a mean single-position posting life of 9.8 days ([St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2017/november/how-quickly-people-apply-jobs)) — giving ≤48h → 5, 48–96h → 3, 4–10 days → 1, >10 days → 0, with the 5/3/1/0 spacing tagged `[assumption]`. **Freshness explicitly does not score the user's odds**, because the evidence that it would is not usable: the "2–3x more interviews for applying inside 48 hours" figure is one unpublished survey by a vendor selling application speed, and "90% of candidates who got interviews applied in the first 24 hours" is pool composition rather than a rate — with 41% of all applications arriving inside 48 hours, that is §10.5's self-confirming measurement again. **The date problem is the substantive half.** Greenhouse's `updated_at` moves on an edit or a repost, Ashby publishes a real publish date, and a DOM-extracted page may have neither, so every job records `dateKind: 'posted' | 'updated' | 'none'`: `none` scores 0 and says so, `updated` caps at 3 and names the field in the score line, and two new CI-blocking invariants (§9.3's shape) enforce both. §7.2's freshness tiebreak is narrowed to raw timestamps within one `dateKind`, falling through to company order across kinds — otherwise the brief ranks by which ATS the company bought. Weights untouched, so §9.1 still sums to 100 and §9.3 invariant 2 stands (both rechecked). **Do not re-propose cutting freshness and redistributing the 5**: that branch is closed, and it would break invariant 2 in one direction or the other. The open follow-on belongs to B-11, which should now also record how many of each board's jobs carry only a mutable date.
