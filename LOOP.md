# Hoshi Loop — operating doc

Read this first, every run. It decides what today's run does.

Hoshi is Dhwani's Chrome extension (Manifest V3) for job search: it recognises job pages, extracts postings, fills applications without ever submitting them, and tracks the people worth talking to. She is a UX Research & Design master's student at UMSI targeting product design and UX research roles, and she needs visa sponsorship.

**The job of this loop is to make the PRD sharper every day, and to make Hoshi the best job-finding tool by the definition in PRD §5.**

## What "improve the PRD" means

**It means sharper, not longer.**

- A run that deletes a vague paragraph and replaces it with a testable sentence is a good run.
- A run that adds a section nobody asked for is a bad run, even if the section is well written.
- Every factual claim needs a source link or an explicit `[assumption]` tag. Untagged assumptions are the failure mode that makes a spec feel authoritative when it is guessing. **As of 2026-10-25 a source is cited inline, at the claim it grounds, with its interest disclosed there — the PRD has no bibliography and must not grow one back (R-14).**
- If two sections say the same thing, merge them.
- If a requirement cannot be tested, either make it testable or cut it.
- **A research finding that produces no requirement is dead weight.** Added 2026-10-10. The 10-04 cut established that §2's rows were the weakest part of the document precisely because none of them changed a requirement; the 10-10 run therefore stopped at the point where its finding produced one (PRD §9.4's provenance rule) rather than at the point where it had an interesting fact. Saturday's job is not "learn something about a competitor". It is "change something in this document because of what a competitor does".
- **The PRD asserts the current product, not its history.** Added 2026-10-11 after R-03 cut §9.1's "Was → Now → Why it moved" table. When a run changes a number or a rule, the new value goes in the PRD and the diff goes in that day's brief and the backlog entry — never into a column, a strikethrough or a parenthetical kept alongside the live value. A PRD that carries its own changelog grows by one line every time it is corrected, and Sunday ends up cutting the record of why Sunday cut things. Dated section headers and the `[assumption]` tags stay; they are current state, not history.
- **An example earns its lines when it is normative.** Added 2026-10-18, as the test that chose that day's cut over the larger one available. §8.3's adapter interface and §9.4's score lines look like illustrations and are not — they state requirements in the only form those requirements have, and cutting them would delete a rule. §7.4's rendered brief is the one block in the document where nothing in it can be failed, which makes it the first example a future cut should take and the last thing to mistake for a requirement. Ask of an example: could an implementation be wrong against it? If not, it is decoration, however good it looks — and decoration is cut before prose that carries a rule. *(The brief mock was duly cut on 2026-10-19 as R-08, the morning after it was spared, when §8.9 forced the ceiling. The test outlives it: apply it to the next candidate.)* Extended 2026-10-22 (R-12) from examples to prose, and again 2026-10-23 (R-13) to arithmetic: **a demonstration that a constraint does not bind is decoration too.** §8.9's volume figures proved that provider volume never mattered, which nothing can be wrong against, so the section kept the finding and the backlog kept the working. Extended again 2026-10-25 (R-14) to the document's *apparatus*: **a structure that is a second copy of the body is decoration at its largest.** The Sources bibliography carried 28 links that were all already inline at the claims they ground, so nothing could be wrong against it and every other cut had to spend a sentence re-syncing it. When looking for the weakest thing in the document, ask the decoration question of whole structures and not only of paragraphs — the big ones hide by reading as infrastructure.

**Target length: under 400 lines.** If the PRD is over that, Sunday's cut is mandatory regardless of what else is queued.

If a claim needs a live request to verify, tag it `[assumption]`, say in the PRD that it is unverified, and put the verification step in the backlog for Dhwani. Do not quietly assert it.

So: a run cannot *count* on reading a page end-to-end, but it must not assume it cannot either. Attempt the fetch; if it times out, plan around search results and pages already quoted in the PRD, and tag the claim rather than citing a link the run never opened. Note that web search in this sandbox returns titles and URLs without snippets, so search alone rarely grounds a claim — the fetch is usually the only way to get a quotable sentence. Amended 2026-10-19: the refusals are not evenly distributed, and in a way that is worth planning around. On both 10-12 and 10-19 the three provider docs (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby) refused while third-party pages about those same APIs went through on the first attempt. So when a primary doc refuses twice across runs, stop re-attempting it every Monday — reach for a page that *quotes* it, cite that page with its interest disclosed, and tag what rests on the quotation. Amended 2026-10-23: the mirror image is worth knowing too. A **competitor's own help centre** has gone through on the first attempt on 10-22 and 10-23 (Simplify, Teal), so the cheapest primary source available to this loop is the rival product's documentation of its own behaviour — better evidence than any review, and it is what grounded §11 and §10.5's B-20 block. Amended 2026-10-30: **a competitor's own marketing and product pages go through too**, and they are primary for what the product claims to do (Jobright's referral page on 10-24, Huntr's tracker page and metrics writeup on 10-30). The reach is now reliable enough that a Saturday or a Friday should assume it has primary vendor material available and plan the run around reading it, rather than around review aggregation.
- **Arithmetic in the PRD gets rechecked, not retyped.** Added 2026-10-09. Every number the document asserts about its own maths — the power calculations in §10.1, the Wilson intervals in §10.3, the weight sums and invariants in §9 — is reproducible from the script in the 2026-10-02 brief or from three lines of Python. A run that moves, quotes or adds one of those numbers runs the check first. The sandbox has Python; this costs seconds and it is the one class of claim the loop can verify by itself rather than tag. Amended 2026-10-11: this is not a formality. The 10-11 run rechecked §9's weights only because a cut moved them, and found that §9.3 invariant 2 had claimed `20 > 20` since 2026-09-29 — in a clause the PRD says should be CI-blocking. **A relational claim counts as arithmetic.** Recheck the inequality, not only the sum. Amended 2026-10-25: **recheck the numbers the run did not touch, too, and state a figure to the precision its inputs support.** That Sunday's cut touched no arithmetic, ran the standard set anyway as the 10-18 run did, and found §10.5's storage figure asserting "under 4%" on a serialisation estimate whose inputs — identifier length, JSON whitespace, how many rows a brief carries — are pinned nowhere; three runs had produced three different answers between 387 and 506 KB, each correct for its own assumptions. The fix is a range (390–510 KB, under 5%) rather than a new point estimate. A number whose inputs are unstated is a false precision even when every digit of the calculation is right. **Amended 2026-10-30: a bound on a figure the loop simulated has to survive the next field someone adds to the schema.** B-21 added one field to every `submission` row and the top of the same sweep went to exactly 5.00%, so the five-day-old "under 5%" was false by the end of the run that had to state it — now *under 6%*. The lesson is not to pick looser bounds for their own sake; it is that **a schema change is an arithmetic change**, so a run that touches a schema reruns every figure computed over it, and states the bound with room for the next field rather than at the current maximum.
- **A share of the successes is not a rate, and a vendor's number about its own product category is not evidence.** Added 2026-10-13, after the freshness research turned up "90% of candidates who got interviews applied in the first 24 hours" — which, against the 41% of all applications that arrive inside 48 hours, is mostly the arrival distribution, and is the same self-confirming shape PRD §10.5 already describes. Before a run puts an outcome statistic in the PRD, it checks two things: what the denominator is (successes, or opportunities?), and who benefits if the number is true. A statistic published by a tool that sells the behaviour the statistic recommends gets the §1 HiringReach treatment — cite it, disclose the interest, tag it — or it stays out. Amended 2026-10-17: a third check, cheaper than both. **Would the requirement change if the number were half what it says?** The ghost-job statistics refused that day (R-06) failed this one — §8.8 acts on a board that stopped listing a job whether the market is 5% ghosts or 40%. A statistic that cannot move a requirement does not need to be adjudicated, only left out. *(Used again on 2026-10-23 to decide not to research what share of applications a seeker sends outside her tracking tool: honesty rule 10 holds at 10% or at 50%, so the number was never worth the fetch. And again on 2026-10-25, in the other direction: the storage figure could not move the no-retention rule either, so it needed de-precising rather than adjudicating — the check decides how hard to work on a number, not only whether to keep it.)*
- **A platform limit is a measurable fact, so measure against it before designing around it.** Added 2026-10-16. B-16 carried a retention question for a week on the intuition that an append-only log grows without bound. It does: to about 400 KB a year, against a 10,485,760-byte `chrome.storage.local` quota — so the honest answer was *no retention rule*, and the week of carrying it was spent on a problem that does not exist. The same four lines of Python found the real one in the job store's `descriptionHtml`. When a constraint is a documented number (a quota, a rate limit, a page cap), fetch the number and run the arithmetic before specifying anything that manages it. Designing a mechanism for a limit nobody has measured is the storage-shaped version of an untagged assumption. **Amended 2026-10-19: a null result is a finding, and an absent limit is not permission.** B-18 was filed as blocked on reading the providers' rate limits, and none of the three documented providers publishes one — so the dependency could never have delivered what the item was waiting for, and two days were spent on that premise. When the documented number does not exist, say so in the PRD with the sourcing for its absence, then name the limit that *does* bind and design against that one instead; §8.9's binding constraints turned out to be the brief's once-a-morning cadence and the MV3 service worker's own ceilings, neither of which is a provider's business. The failure mode this prevents is the opposite of the 10-16 one: not designing a mechanism for an unmeasured limit, but helping yourself to an unbounded one because nobody wrote a number down. **Amended 2026-10-25: the quota is measured, the thing filling it is modelled, and only one of those is a fact.** 10,485,760 bytes is documented; what a year of events serialises to is a simulation of a schema nobody has run, so it is the *side of the comparison that needs the range*, and B-11's real response bodies are the only thing that turns it into a measurement.
- **A principle that refuses an observation is also a constraint on the metrics.** Added 2026-10-23, after three runs in a row found the same class of error by accident. §10.3's rules 8 and 9 were written by looking at the data (does this value mutate? is this event mature?), and both missed the cheaper question: *which side of a Principle does this event happen on?* Principle 4 refuses her mail, so 10-21 found the thesis' own numerator had no record at all. Principle 1 refuses the submit, so 10-23 found three metrics written past Hoshi's last observable moment. The rule for future runs: **when a run adds or moves a count or a rate, it names where the event happens** — inside Hoshi, or somewhere a Principle has refused to go — and if it is the latter, the metric is the user's assertion and says so, with the direction of its skew (PRD §10.3 rule 10). This is also the cheapest way to audit the document: take one of its refusals and ask what is being claimed on the far side of it. **Amended 2026-10-30: the far side of a refusal is also where the only fix lives, and the fix is a form.** Rule 10's coverage gap cannot be closed by Hoshi observing more, because a Principle is what puts those events out of reach — so the remedy for every one of these is the user entering the row herself (B-21 for a submission, B-10 for a person), and such a row is specified by **what it does not carry**. A manually entered row that carries everything an observed one carries is a fabrication with a nicer provenance.
- **A verb is a capability claim, and the PRD must own one for every verb it gives Hoshi.** Added 2026-10-24, when yesterday's audit rule was pointed at §8.2 and §8.6 rather than at a Principle and found §9.2 claiming Hoshi *surfaced* and *sourced* a person — which no endpoint, store or permission in the document can do. The tell is cheap to look for: wherever the PRD writes *Hoshi finds / surfaces / sources / detects* something, name the endpoint, store or permission that produces it, and if none exists, the sentence is describing a competitor's architecture rather than this one. **And when a display line and a definition disagree about who did something, the display line is usually right** — §9.4's score line said *from **your** ranked alum list* while §9.2's definition said Hoshi surfaced them, and the line the user reads was the one written honestly, because someone had to imagine her reading it.
- **The audit rule works on the document's apparatus, not only on its claims.** Added 2026-10-25, which is the third consecutive run to find its work by pointing an existing rule somewhere it had not been pointed. 10-23 pointed rule 8 at a Principle, 10-24 pointed the audit rule at §8.2 and §8.6, and 10-25 pointed the decoration test at the *structures* of the document rather than at its sentences — which found a 28-line bibliography that was a copy of the body and that eleven previous cuts had walked past, because it reads as infrastructure and infrastructure is not where anyone looks for dead weight. **So on a Sunday, before hunting for the weakest paragraph, ask the decoration question of every whole block: the tables, the code fences, the lists, the headers, the apparatus.** The largest cut available is usually a structure, not a sentence, and the reason it is still there is that it does not look like content.
- **A spawned item usually carries its own specification, so read the filing before re-deriving it.** Added 2026-10-30. B-21 was opened on 10-23 with its schema, its two denominators and its reason for not being folded in already written down by the run that walked into it, because that run understood the problem better in the moment than a later run would from the PRD alone. Today's work was therefore not design but **the two things the filing had left open** — the direction of the new bias, and the reason to ship a feature that makes the headline metric look worse. So when a Done entry says *spawned B-N, deliberately not folded in*, the filing is a handover and not a title: start from what it already decided, and spend the run on the part it could not.
- **A stored fact has a clock, even when nothing in the product moves it.** Added 2026-10-31, which found its work by pointing the audit rule at the document's *fields* rather than at its verbs: which stored values go false while nobody touches them? The document already handles two kinds of staleness and this is a third. Rule 8 catches a value the **product** mutates after the event it describes (a tier that decays). §9.5 catches a date the **provider** controls and may move (an `updated_at`). Neither covers a value the **world** falsifies while both Hoshi and the user leave the record alone — and that was the person record's employer, the single fact §8.1's watchlist, every tier in §9.2 and 20 of §9.1's 100 points all rest on, carried with no date at all. So of every stored assertion, ask what would have to happen outside Hoshi for it to become false; if the answer is ordinary life — someone changes jobs, graduates, moves — the record carries the date the user last asserted it and the line that spends it prints that date. **The cheap tell is a field with no sibling date**, which is a question that can be asked of a schema in a minute and had never been asked of this one.

Keep it short. She reads these on a phone.

```markdown
# YYYY-MM-DD

- <one line per change, naming the PRD section or backlog item>

## What I found
<only if research happened — the finding and its source link>

```

Do not pad a quiet day. "Cut §4 from eleven lines to four, no other changes" is a complete brief.
