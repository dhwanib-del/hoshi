# Hoshi Loop — operating doc

Read this first, every run. It decides what today's run does.

Hoshi is Dhwani's Chrome extension (Manifest V3) for job search: it recognises job pages, extracts postings, fills applications without ever submitting them, and tracks the people worth talking to. She is a UX Research & Design master's student at UMSI targeting product design and UX research roles, and she needs visa sponsorship.

**The job of this loop is to make the PRD sharper every day, and to make Hoshi the best job-finding tool by the definition in PRD §5.**

0. **Look at the working directory first: `ls` and `git log --oneline -10`.** Added 2026-10-30, the morning a misdirected upload revealed that Dhwani had pushed the extension codebase on 10-28 and two runs had specified past it. The Project is not the only thing that changes between runs, and a loop that reads only its own documents will keep believing whatever they last said about the world.

2. **Read `claude/hoshi/BACKLOG.md`.** Never re-propose anything under Done or Rejected.

6. **Write today's brief** to `claude/hoshi/briefs/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
7. **Update the PRD and BACKLOG** with what changed.
8. **Finish with a SendUserMessage**: what changed today in one line each, and the single most useful thing she could answer.

One substantive change per run. Not three, not ten. A loop that touches everything daily produces a document nobody can read by week three.

Choose in this order:

2. **If a high-priority backlog item is ready** → do that one. Take it from Open to Done with the actual specification written into the PRD, not a note saying it should be.

## What "improve the PRD" means

**It means sharper, not longer.**

- A run that deletes a vague paragraph and replaces it with a testable sentence is a good run.
- A run that adds a section nobody asked for is a bad run, even if the section is well written.
- Every factual claim needs a source link or an explicit `[assumption]` tag. Untagged assumptions are the failure mode that makes a spec feel authoritative when it is guessing. **As of 2026-10-25 a source is cited inline, at the claim it grounds, with its interest disclosed there — the PRD has no bibliography and must not grow one back (R-14).**
- If two sections say the same thing, merge them.
- If a requirement cannot be tested, either make it testable or cut it.
- **A research finding that produces no requirement is dead weight.** Added 2026-10-10. The 10-04 cut established that §2's rows were the weakest part of the document precisely because none of them changed a requirement; the 10-10 run therefore stopped at the point where its finding produced one (PRD §9.4's provenance rule) rather than at the point where it had an interesting fact. Saturday's job is not "learn something about a competitor". It is "change something in this document because of what a competitor does".
- **The PRD asserts the current product, not its history.** Added 2026-10-11 after R-03 cut §9.1's "Was → Now → Why it moved" table. When a run changes a number or a rule, the new value goes in the PRD and the diff goes in that day's brief and the backlog entry — never into a column, a strikethrough or a parenthetical kept alongside the live value. A PRD that carries its own changelog grows by one line every time it is corrected, and Sunday ends up cutting the record of why Sunday cut things. Dated section headers and the `[assumption]` tags stay; they are current state, not history.
- **An example earns its lines when it is normative.** Added 2026-10-18, as the test that chose that day's cut over the larger one available. §8.3's adapter interface and §9.4's score lines look like illustrations and are not — they state requirements in the only form those requirements have, and cutting them would delete a rule. §7.4's rendered brief is the one block in the document where nothing in it can be failed, which makes it the first example a future cut should take and the last thing to mistake for a requirement. Ask of an example: could an implementation be wrong against it? If not, it is decoration, however good it looks — and decoration is cut before prose that carries a rule. *(The brief mock was duly cut on 2026-10-19 as R-08, the morning after it was spared, when §8.9 forced the ceiling. The test outlives it: apply it to the next candidate.)* Extended 2026-10-22 (R-12) from examples to prose, and again 2026-10-23 (R-13) to arithmetic: **a demonstration that a constraint does not bind is decoration too.** §8.9's volume figures proved that provider volume never mattered, which nothing can be wrong against, so the section kept the finding and the backlog kept the working. Extended again 2026-10-25 (R-14) to the document's *apparatus*: **a structure that is a second copy of the body is decoration at its largest.** The Sources bibliography carried 28 links that were all already inline at the claims they ground, so nothing could be wrong against it and every other cut had to spend a sentence re-syncing it. When looking for the weakest thing in the document, ask the decoration question of whole structures and not only of paragraphs — the big ones hide by reading as infrastructure.

**Target length: under 400 lines.** If the PRD is over that, Sunday's cut is mandatory regardless of what else is queued.

Amended 2026-10-25: **recheck the numbers the run did not touch, too, and state a figure to the precision its inputs support.** That Sunday's cut touched no arithmetic, ran the standard set anyway as the 10-18 run did, and found §10.5's storage figure asserting "under 4%" on a serialisation estimate whose inputs — identifier length, JSON whitespace, how many rows a brief carries — are pinned nowhere; three runs had produced three different answers between 387 and 506 KB, each correct for its own assumptions. - **A platform limit is a measurable fact, so measure against it before designing around it.** Added 2026-10-16. Designing a mechanism for a limit nobody has measured is the storage-shaped version of an untagged assumption. **Amended 2026-10-19: a null result is a finding, and an absent limit is not permission.** B-18 was filed as blocked on reading the providers' rate limits, and none of the three documented providers publishes one — so the dependency could never have delivered what the item was waiting for, and two days were spent on that premise. - **The audit rule works on the document's apparatus, not only on its claims.** Added 2026-10-25, which is the third consecutive run to find its work by pointing an existing rule somewhere it had not been pointed. 10-23 pointed rule 8 at a Principle, 10-24 pointed the audit rule at §8.2 and §8.6, and 10-25 pointed the decoration test at the *structures* of the document rather than at its sentences — which found a 28-line bibliography that was a copy of the body and that eleven previous cuts had walked past, because it reads as infrastructure and infrastructure is not where anyone looks for dead weight. **So on a Sunday, before hunting for the weakest paragraph, ask the decoration question of every whole block: the tables, the code fences, the lists, the headers, the apparatus.** The largest cut available is usually a structure, not a sentence, and the reason it is still there is that it does not look like content.
- **A spawned item usually carries its own specification, so read the filing before re-deriving it.** Added 2026-10-30. B-21 was opened on 10-23 with its schema, its two denominators and its reason for not being folded in already written down by the run that walked into it, because that run understood the problem better in the moment than a later run would from the PRD alone. So when a Done entry says *spawned B-N, deliberately not folded in*, the filing is a handover and not a title: start from what it already decided, and spend the run on the part it could not.
- **A stored fact has a clock, even when nothing in the product moves it.** Added 2026-10-31, which found its work by pointing the audit rule at the document's *fields* rather than at its verbs: which stored values go false while nobody touches them? The document already handles two kinds of staleness and this is a third. Rule 8 catches a value the **product** mutates after the event it describes (a tier that decays). §9.5 catches a date the **provider** controls and may move (an `updated_at`). Neither covers a value the **world** falsifies while both Hoshi and the user leave the record alone — and that was the person record's employer, the single fact §8.1's watchlist, every tier in §9.2 and 20 of §9.1's 100 points all rest on, carried with no date at all. So of every stored assertion, ask what would have to happen outside Hoshi for it to become false; if the answer is ordinary life — someone changes jobs, graduates, moves — the record carries the date the user last asserted it and the line that spends it prints that date. **The cheap tell is a field with no sibling date**, which is a question that can be asked of a schema in a minute and had never been asked of this one.
- **A shared shape is only as specified as its least-specified producer.** Added 2026-11-02, which found its work by pointing the audit rule at the document's *join keys* — the fourth consecutive family member after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25) and fields-without-dates (10-31). §8.4 opens by saying one normaliser serves the page path and the adapter path, which reads as both paths filling the same fields, and then specifies the key names for the adapters only. So `providerJobId` — which §8.5 matches on and §8.8 closes jobs by — was required of one producer and never asked of the other, and the gap was invisible because the sentence promising a shared shape sits directly above the half that was specified. The tell is cheap and sits beside 10-31's field-with-no-sibling-date: **a key the document matches on, with only one of the paths that write it specified.** So of any shape the PRD says two producers share, ask of each field which producers must fill it; and of any key the PRD matches on, ask whether every record that could be matched is required to carry one. Where the answer is no, the match silently becomes a miss, and a miss in a *negative* observation — absence, closure, exclusion from a denominator — reads as evidence rather than as a failure to compare.

- **A field nothing reads is where a literal survives every test, and in an append-only store its staleness is permanent.** Added 2026-11-05, the sixth family member of the audit rule after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25), undated fields (10-31), join keys (11-02), constants (11-03) and the loop's own previous change (11-04). The 11-04 run derived every *scored* figure from the weight table and asserted eight relations in CI, and it could not have caught `weightsVersion`, because a test asserts over values something consumes and that field is consumed by nothing — it is written for a segmentation rule 11 has not built yet. So the cheap tell, askable of any schema in a minute: **which fields are written and never read?** Those are the ones where a literal reads as authoritative for as long as nobody looks. The second half decides *priority*, not just discovery: when a run finds a defect in what gets **written** and a defect in what gets **displayed**, the write-path one goes first, because a display bug is fixed by the next release while a row written wrong into an append-only log is wrong for ever — and the only record of last season is made of those rows. The fix shape is invariant (13)'s, moved from a quantity to an identity: derive the value from the thing it describes, carry it with that thing rather than stamping it at the write site, and **refuse the write when it is absent rather than defaulting** — fail closed means writing nothing, not writing a guess.

- **A rule with an escape hatch is the escape hatch, so check which branch the product's own volumes force.** Added 2026-11-06, the seventh family member of the audit rule after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25), undated fields (10-31), join keys (11-02), constants (11-03), the loop's own previous change (11-04) and write-only fields (11-05). Honesty rule 11 read *Activity segments every rate by `source` **or says that it did not***, which looks like a rule with a graceful fallback and is in fact a rule plus permission to skip it. The arithmetic decides which: splitting a denominator leaves the smaller arm under rule 1's floor of 10 until that arm **by itself** reaches 10 — 40 submissions at a one-in-four hand-entry share, 100 at one in ten — so against §10.1's volumes the disclaimer branch is what the product takes all season, and the rule's real content is a caveat printed under a number already read. **The tell is a disjunction in a requirement** (*segments … or states that it did not*, *verifies … or flags*), and the question to ask of it is not whether the second branch is honest but **how often the document's own numbers force it**. Where the cheap branch is the default, withdraw it. **And the replacement fallback is one an existing rule already established, never a new and laxer one:** rule 1 had long since settled what to show when n cannot support an estimate — the fraction — so rule 11's small-n case uses rule 1's answer rather than inventing *pool it and apologise*. A second fallback for the same situation is two standards wearing one rule's number.

- **A term with a weight and no rule is where the document never looked, and a 0 that means *not found* is scored as evidence.** Added 2026-11-07, the eighth family member of the audit rule after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25), undated fields (10-31), join keys (11-02), constants (11-03), the loop's own previous change (11-04), write-only fields (11-05) and disjunctions (11-06). The tell is the cheapest in the family: **take the weight table and ask, of each term, which section computes it.** Connection had §9.2, freshness had §9.5, the gates had §9.1's three states — and role, the largest term at 25 of the 45-wide ranked range and the one §9.3 invariant 1's floor gates on, had a number and nothing else for nine runs of §9. A term's *weight* is so visibly specified that nobody checks whether its *value* is, and the biggest term is the least likely to be suspected. The second half is the reusable one and it generalises past scoring: **a 0 produced by "nothing matched" is not the same claim as a 0 produced by "no", and a threshold cannot tell them apart.** §8.8 already settled this for a listing state — a failure to observe is not an observation of absence — and the same sentence was false one domain over, in a score, where an unrecognised job title dropped below the floor exactly as an unsuitable role does. So of any term with a lowest band, ask what produces that band: if the answer is *Hoshi did not find anything*, the band is a measurement failure wearing a verdict's clothes. The fix is not to promote it — §9.1's held state floods when the unmatched set is a whole board's engineering roles — but to **make the exclusion countable**, in rule 6's shape, on the one surface where the user could notice it was wrong.

- **The document's claims about itself go stale invisibly, and the modest direction is the one nobody audits.** Added 2026-11-08, the ninth family member of the audit rule after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25), undated fields (10-31), join keys (11-02), constants (11-03), the loop's own previous change (11-04), write-only fields (11-05), disjunctions (11-06) and weighted terms with no rule (11-07) — and the first one found by pointing the rule at the PRD's statements about the PRD. Four engine sections each opened with *Specified <date> (B-NN). No code written.* and §4 enumerated the same five under the same claim; all four of the *no code written* clauses had been false since 11-03, when this loop began shipping code, and **nothing failed, because a section's lead line is read by nothing** — 11-05's tell applied to prose instead of to a schema field. **The tell is a sentence whose subject is the document or the repository rather than the product** (*specified on*, *no code written*, *extended with*, *this section replaces*), and the rule is that such a sentence belongs in that day's brief and backlog entry, never in the PRD. **The reusable half is the direction.** The hard constraint *do not invent build status* was written to stop the loop claiming **more** than exists; it does not catch the loop claiming **less**, and an underclaim reads as modesty rather than as an error, which is why four copies survived five days while the fifth — §4's, the only one a reader checks — was the one the loop is forbidden to touch. So when an honesty rule guards one direction, ask what the same sentence looks like pointing the other way, and check whether anything in the document would notice.

- **A requirement written about one direction of an inference leaves the other direction to whoever writes the record.** Added 2026-11-09, the tenth family member of the audit rule after verbs (10-24), apparatus (10-25), undated fields (10-31), join keys (11-02), constants (11-03), the loop's own previous change (11-04), write-only fields (11-05), disjunctions (11-06), weighted terms with no rule (11-07) and the document's claims about itself (11-08) — and it is the second consecutive run whose finding is a *direction*. §8.8 is named for a negative — *absence is evidence, but only from a complete response* — and it specified closure thoroughly, down to making it ignore which path created the record (11-02). It never said what the **same response** proves by **presence**, so of a tri-state's transitions two were observed and the third was a field the producer stamped when it minted the row: `live` meant *an adapter made this record*, not *the board says it is listed*, and a job she had opened at a watched board stayed `unknown` through every morning that board confirmed it while the same response was trusted to close it. **The tell is a requirement whose subject is a failure, an absence or an exclusion**, and the question to ask of it is what the opposite observation establishes and **who writes that value today**.
