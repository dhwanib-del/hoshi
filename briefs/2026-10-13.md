## What I found

The honest version of freshness is smaller than the category's version, and the gap is a measurement error I have already written a section about.

What is solid: DHI Group, linking nearly 80 million applications to almost 10 million postings, found **41% of applications arrive in the first 48 hours and 56% within 96**, with a mean life of **9.8 days** for a single-position posting ([St. Louis Fed](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2017/november/how-quickly-people-apply-jobs)). That is a real, large, independent dataset, and it licenses exactly one claim — about queue length. It says nothing about whether applying early works.

What is not solid is everything the category builds on top of it. The "apply within 24 hours and get 2–3x more interviews" figure, and the day-band index under it (Day 1-2 = 2.5x average, Day 15+ = 0.1x), trace to a single unpublished survey by a job-application tool — a vendor whose product is speed, which is the same conflict §1 already discloses for HiringReach. And the most-quoted line of all, *"90% of candidates who secured interviews applied within the first 24 hours"*, is not a rate at all: it is a share of the successes. With 41% of all applications arriving inside 48 hours, an early-heavy group of interviewees is substantially just the arrival distribution ([jobstrack collects both](https://jobstrack.io/blog/first-mover-advantage-applying-early-tech-jobs)). That is §10.5's self-confirming measurement wearing a different hat — and it is why §9.5 says in so many words that freshness does not score your chances.

The second thing I did not expect: freshness was going to be dishonest in Hoshi too, and §8.4 already knew it. Greenhouse hands back `updated_at`, so a role posted in June and edited yesterday would have scored the full 5 and told you the pool was 59% empty. That is the one place a 5-point term could do real damage, which is why the cap and the `none` → 0 rule are invariants rather than guidance.
