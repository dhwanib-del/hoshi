**Focus:** Saturday — competitive research. The finding is structural, not a feature: Hoshi already collects a signal the whole category pays to approximate, and §8 was throwing it away.

## What changed

- **PRD gains §8.8, "Delisting: absence is evidence, but only from a complete response."** A job missing from a board response with `complete === true` becomes `closed`, recording `closedAt` and `closedReason: 'delisted'`.
- **The negative case is the substantive half.** A job missing from a response that is incomplete, truncated, rate-limited, schema-mismatched or errored closes **nothing** — that is a failure to observe, not an observation of absence. Principle 5 applied to a negative, which §8.3's fail-closed rule had only ever covered in the positive direction.
- **Three listing states, never two:** `live | closed | unknown`. A page-extracted job at a person-only company (§8.7) has no board to be absent from, so it stays `unknown` and is never displayed as either of the others.
- **Delisting writes no `outcome` event** (§10.5). A posting coming down says nothing about your application, and logging it as `rejected` would fabricate a result into an append-only store that cannot take it back.
- **§9.5's `> 10 days → 0` band corrected.** It read as though age implied the role might be gone. Age scores how full the pool is; closure is now observed. A `live` job with freshness 0 is still live.
- **B-18 opened** (medium, mine not yours): discovery has no polling cadence, and delisting latency is now exactly that interval. Blocked on B-11 — picking an interval without knowing the providers' rate limits is designing around an unmeasured limit.
- **B-17 gets its first free eviction candidate**: a `closed` job's `descriptionHtml` is bytes nobody can apply to.

## What I found

The competitive gap is not a feature anyone shipped, it is a structural property of where the data comes from. Aggregators crawl, and *"nothing notifies the crawler when a role closes"*; the recheck loop is expensive, which *"is why expired postings linger on so many sites"*, while *"the ATS record is what the employer edits, so it reflects status changes fastest"* ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/job-aggregator-sites) — a seller of ATS feed data, so tagged, though the mechanism is not in dispute and Greenhouse corroborates it by leaving a tombstone page at the old URL). The line that stung: *"an aggregator that ages listings out on a timer is guessing; one that rechecks the source knows."* §8.1 polls the source already. §9.5 was ageing listings out on a timer. Hoshi was paying for the good data and then guessing anyway.

**Two ghost-job statistics were refused rather than used.** ResumeUp.AI's widely quoted *27.4% of US LinkedIn listings are likely ghost jobs* counts postings older than 30 days and calls that intent — a proxy wearing a conclusion's clothes, from a career-site vendor, with no published sample size ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/one-quarter-of-jobs-posted-online-are-fake-ghost-jobs-study/496683)). Greenhouse's own 18–22% of postings leading to no hire is better sourced, since those are outcomes inside its own ATS ([Fox 35](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/ghost-jobs-greenhouse-analysis)), but Greenhouse sells hiring software and runs a verification programme, and more to the point the number changes nothing: §8.8 does not need to know what share of the market is ghosts to act on a board that stopped listing a job. Both are recorded as refused in R-06 so a future run does not prop up §8.8 with one.

I also checked whether Greenhouse Verified could feed §9's scoring. It cannot — it certifies the employer's use of Greenhouse features (inclusion reminders, candidate surveys), not whether a role is real, and nothing in the support docs says it is exposed in the board API.

Nothing to apply to the codebase.
