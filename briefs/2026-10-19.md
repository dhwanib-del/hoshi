## What I found

Three providers, same answer. Greenhouse *"publishes no rate limit for its GET endpoints"*; Ashby *"publishes no rate limit for the public job posting endpoint"*; Lever *"publishes no rate limit for reading postings"*, and its one published figure — 2 requests per second — governs application POSTs, a different endpoint ([JobsPipe source notes](https://jobspipe.dev/sources/lever.md), per provider; a seller of ATS feed data, so tagged where it advises rather than reports). Workday documents no API at all. The three provider docs themselves refused to open from the sandbox again today, which is why the sourcing is one step removed and tagged.

The measurable limits turned out to be on Hoshi's side of the wire: an extension service worker is terminated after 30 seconds of inactivity, if one request runs past 5 minutes, or if a single `fetch` takes more than 30 seconds to answer ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle)), and `chrome.alarms` won't honour a period under 0.5 minutes in a packed extension ([alarms reference](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv2/reference/alarms)). That is what shaped the per-board-resume design, not politeness toward the providers.

The loop rule this produced: a null result is a finding. The 10-16 constraint said to fetch the documented number before designing around a limit; it now also says that when the number doesn't exist, you say so and name the limit that *does* bind, rather than treating the absence as freedom. B-18 had been carried as blocked for two days on a dependency that could not have delivered what it was asked for — same shape as B-16's retention question, which was carried for a week on an intuition the arithmetic didn't support.
