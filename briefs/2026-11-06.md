## What I found

Activity built every rate over `events.filter(x => x.kind === 'submission')` — both arms, every weight table, one denominator — and then labelled the first one *"Of applications marked submitted in Hoshi"* over a set that included your hand-entered rows. The component whose entire job is to state its population stated the wrong one, confidently. A label is also the only part of a rate you cannot check: you can recompute `30%` from `6 of 20`, and there is nothing on the screen from which to recompute *of the 24 applications Hoshi filled*.

The better finding is rule 11. It read *segments every rate by `source` **or says that it did not***, which reads like a rule with a graceful fallback and is actually a rule plus permission to skip it — and the arithmetic says which branch you get. Splitting a denominator leaves the smaller arm under rule 1's floor of 10 until that arm *by itself* reaches 10: **40 submissions at a one-in-four hand-entry share, 100 at one in ten.** At the volumes §10.1 describes, "we did not segment" was never the edge case. It was the all-season default, printing its caveat underneath a number you had already read.

So the hatch is withdrawn, and the replacement is a fallback the document already owned — rule 1 settled years of this argument with *show `3 of 7`, never `43%`*. Inventing *pool it and apologise* as a second answer to the same situation was two standards sharing one rule's number.
