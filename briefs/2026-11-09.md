## What I found

I pointed the audit rule at the document's **state machine**: of the three listing states, which transitions does anything actually observe? `closed` is observed (absence from a complete response). `unknown` is observed (no board, no comparable id, a stale board). **`live` was not observed at all** — it was a field the adapter wrote when it minted a row, and nothing ever re-wrote it. I ran the current code to be sure rather than reading it: a page-captured job sat through **four consecutive complete responses that contained it** and stayed `unknown`, badged *Listing unknown* in the panel, while the fifth response — the one that dropped it — closed it immediately. The board's answer was trusted to take a job away from you and not to confirm it.

The reusable half is the direction again, one day after the same lesson about the document's claims about itself. §8.8 is named for a negative (*absence is evidence*), and a rule written about one direction of an inference leaves the other direction to whoever writes the record — which here was the producer, with nothing behind it. The tell is cheap: **of every state a record carries, ask which transitions an observation produces and which a producer's default does.** The asserted ones hide because the stored value and the observed value have the same name.
