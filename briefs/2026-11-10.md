## What I found

I pointed the audit rule at a term's **bands** rather than at the term. Friday the 7th specified role fit and put the Principle 6 rule in its first paragraph — *the matched lane is named in the score line or the points are not awarded* — and then defined `lane | adjacent | unmatched` in the same breath. The middle band could never satisfy that rule: `adjacent` paid **16 of 25, one point over the role floor**, off a fixed regex of UX-shaped titles living in `src/scoring.js`, and named nothing.

Two things I ran rather than argued. With a profile holding **no saved lanes at all**, a job still scored 16 and still cleared the floor — so the term you are told you control in Settings had a floor-clearing band that was deaf to your profile. And *Clinical Researcher* cleared the floor on the regex's `researcher`, with no evidence printed anywhere you could have caught it.

The reusable half is why that survives rather than why it happened: the top band obviously names its lane and the bottom band obviously scores nothing, so of any rule shaped *name X or no points*, the question is which **values** of the term can satisfy it — the middle ones are where nobody looks. The second question to ask of such a band is what it is derived *from*. A band computed from a list the product holds, rather than from what you wrote, keeps paying points after you empty the field it claims to read, and that is the cheap test for whether a term is actually yours.
