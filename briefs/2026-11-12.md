## What I found

I pointed the audit rule at an invariant's **enforcement mechanism** rather than at its claim. Invariant (8) cited the walk test Principle 3 puts on the profile. That test finds protected **keys** — which works on the profile, whose keys are fixed, and cannot work on the bank, where the key set is fixed too and the demographic question sits in a value. So the invariant was true, vacuous, and the only real guard was a regex the PRD never mentioned.

Run against the shipped guard: **nine of twenty demographic wordings stored and then filled.** *Are you Hispanic or Latino?* — the ethnicity half of the standard two-question EEO format — *Please select your racial/ethnic category*, *Ethnic background*, *What are your pronouns?*, *Do you identify as LGBTQ+?*, *Do you require any accommodations?*, *What is your age?*, and a UK monitoring form's *long-term physical or mental health condition*. Meanwhile all four wordings an ATS-integration vendor calls typical were caught: *What is your gender?*, *What is your race/ethnicity?*, *Are you a protected veteran?*, *Do you have a disability?* ([Kombo](https://docs.kombo.dev/ats/features/eeo-questions), documenting the field it normalises for Greenhouse and Ashby among others, so tagged past what it reports).

And the existing test enumerated those same four. That is the reusable half: **a test over a deny-list can only assert the entries someone already thought of**, so it transcribes the list rather than checking it, and passes for ever. A deny-list also fails in the opposite direction from everything else in this document — every fail-closed rule here withholds a claim when it fires, while a deny-list's silence is a store and then a fill. Since coverage cannot be tested, what the four new tests assert is the mechanism: the invariant over the store, and what happens when the list changes.
