## What I found

B-27's filing said to settle the dispute by asking which rate a reader of Activity could check — invariant (15)'s test. **That test does not discriminate:** both populations are nameable from their own membership tests (*of the applications Hoshi filled, 21+ days old*, and *of C3 applications Hoshi filled*). So I ran the arithmetic instead, and it chose neither of the filing's two branches.

The filing's second branch rested on §10.4's *far under the 141*. That figure is for detecting a 5% → 15% move — a small effect. The effect the tier comparison exists to see is the one §1 is built on, a 5–10x gap, and at that size the statistics are cheap: 27 rows per arm at the pessimistic corner, 7 at the optimistic one, and as few as 1–4 C3 rows when the C0 arm is large enough to pin its own interval low. **A refusal has to be sized against the effect it refuses to measure.** Nobody audits a *no* for being too strong, which is why this sat for six weeks — the 11-08 lesson (the modest direction is the one nobody audits) one step along.

And the correction is worth less than what it exposes. Rule 3 withholds the causal sentence *until the intervals are disjoint* — so at this effect size the guard **clears inside one season, exactly when the thesis is being measured**, and then stops objecting to *C3 responds better because the connection worked*. A guard whose threshold the product's own ambitions clear is not a guard. The thing it was standing in for has to be said outright, so rule 3 now says it: a self-selected segmentation yields a difference and never an effect.
