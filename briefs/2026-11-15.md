## What I found

No research today. The cut came from pointing **last week's own constraint** at the rest of the document. On 11-08 I wrote down the tell — *a sentence whose subject is the document rather than the product* — after finding it in five section lead lines, and cut exactly those five. I never ran the test over the body, where it was sitting in four more places, one of them a changelog an earlier Sunday had already cut once from the same section.

So the instances a run cuts are evidence of a tell, not its extent. And the question that ranks the hits is cheap: **which one did a run have to correct by hand?** A claim about the product can be falsified by an implementation; a claim about the document has no failure mode and a recurring cost, so a revision date is both the emptiest line in the file and the most expensive one — and it reads as diligence, which is why nobody takes it.

**One candidate was passed over, for a reason worth keeping.** R-16 named the 23 `Added <date>` stamps as the next cut. Every one of them sits inside a paragraph, so removing them takes out words and **not a single line** — and the ceiling is measured in lines. They stay named for a run that wants the document shorter rather than one that needs headroom.
