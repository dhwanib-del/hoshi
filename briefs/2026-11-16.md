## What I found

I did what 11-15's constraint says to do: take the newest rule and run it over the document rather than over the structure that produced it. 11-14's cap tell was born on §7's block 1 and had only ever been pointed at caps on **lists** — 3 conversations, 5 promoted jobs, Workday's 2,000 — each of which has an n+1th row to point at and a number to print. A cap on a **string** has no overflow surface, which is why eleven runs of §8 walked straight past this one.

**The storage arithmetic is why the field is bounded and why I did not simply raise the cap.** Serialising a real record: ~21 KB at a full description → the 10,485,760-byte quota fills at **~495 jobs**, which reproduces B-17's old 200–500 estimate from the other direction; ~2.9 KB at 1,800 characters → **~3,600**; ~5.1 KB at 4,000 → **~2,050**. Twelve boards of 150 jobs is about half the quota and twelve of 400 exceeds it, so §8.1's whole-board subscription still reaches the ceiling. The cap is a real trade and it is still yours, so it did not move.

**The substantive half is §9.6's 0 band a second time.** Portfolio relevance is the only term that reads this field, and it printed *no saved project matches this posting* over text it had only partly seen. A cap can hide a tag and can never invent one, so **truncation produces false negatives only** — the single thing it can corrupt is a negative finding, which is §8.8's *a failure to observe is not an observation of absence* in a third domain.

And the evidence says truncation is the common case rather than the edge. Across 29,000+ postings the median description runs **412 words** (p75 604, p90 853 — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/job-descriptions-analyzed-study/), a recruiting-software vendor reporting its own customers' postings and filtering to ≥500 characters, so tagged: direction, not measurement). Appcast's employer-side data bands postings *by character count*, best band **4,000–5,000**, with 1,000–2,000 characters called **too short** at 170–250 words ([summary](https://jobmarketmonitor.com/2015/03/19/long-or-short-job-descriptions-and-titles-click-to-applies-are-up-to-five-times-higher-for-job-descriptions-between-2000-to-10000-characters) of ~400,000 seekers and 30,051 applications, a recruitment advertiser's own data, tagged where it advises) — which also supplies the 5–8 characters-a-word conversion. At that rate 1,800 characters holds **55–87% of a median posting and 26–42% of a p90 one**. At half the median it would hold a whole one, which is why the figure is in the document rather than left out.
