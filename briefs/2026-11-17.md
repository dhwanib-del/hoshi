## What I found

B-28 was filed on 11-11 with its specification written in, so the run started from what that filing decided and spent itself on the one thing it left open: **which reading, settled by what the score line can honestly print.**

**Two constructions, and the probe is the argument.** A saved, hand-typed contact with one send 40 days unanswered scores **C1 at 6 points** and satisfies neither invariant (5) nor invariant (10) — she has no alum record and no `employerAsOf`. The unsaved ranked alum beside her scores C1 at 6 as well. **Both return the same tier string, the same total (76) and the same stored row** `{contactId, tier:'C1'}`, so nothing downstream could tell them apart — and 11-11's monotone decay made the second population permanent rather than transient.

**The single-construction reading is refused on the record.** It would cost a decayed contact **10 points to 0** for one unanswered message, against the 4 that decay intends, which is §9.1's own argument against ranking an unverified gate word for word: a deficit that is the absence of a reply rather than a property of the path. And C0's line reads *nobody found at this company*, which is false of someone you wrote to.

**Where the damage actually lands is the stored row, which is why that went first** (the 11-05 rule). §10.2's by-tier C1 arm was pooling *a path you never used* with *a path you used and nothing came back* — opposite evidence about the thesis under one label, which is rule 11's own prohibition one dimension over. A pooled display is fixed by a release; a C1 row written into an append-only log without its construction can never be reclassified.

The constraint this adds, for future runs: **a value two different rules can produce is a key with two populations, and its invariants were written for whichever rule came first.** C1 was defined once and specified once as *one source only*, while decay — three paragraphs down — had been producing C1s out of C2s the whole time. The tell: for each value a state can take, list every rule that can produce it.
