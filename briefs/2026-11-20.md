## What I found

**Wednesday asked which value of an enum is the bad news. Today asks which rules read that value.** `outcome.stage` has carried `rejected` since 10-16, and **nothing in §10 read it.** So the hit test was `['response','screen','interview']` and the fourth value fell into the negative branch by default.

**Run rather than argued.** A season of 40 matured applications holding 18 rejections and 2 screens renders `2 of 40 · 5%` under the label *A response* — with 20 outcome rows in the log and 18 of them saying the employer wrote back. Three faults, in order of weight:

1. **It is rule 9's own sentence, violated one half of the fraction along.** Rule 9 excludes recent rows because *absence of an outcome means not yet, not no*. The numerator was treating a no exactly as it treated absence, in the same rate.
2. **It throws away the rows that narrow rule 10's lower bound.** §10.5 says an `outcome` row is the only thing that confirms an application landed, and a rejection is the commonest one there is. Dropping it reads as caution and is the opposite — the discarded rows were the evidence.
3. **It is unrecheckable.** `2 of 40` shows you the denominator and tells you nothing about what counted as a hit. That is 11-06's argument about population labels, one slot over and worse.

**The asymmetry between the two funnels is what made it visible.** Reply rate counts a `declined` row on purpose — §10.5 says so, because that rate measures whether outreach gets answered and never whether it helped. The application funnel's rate silently dropped its own word for a no. Wednesday found the conversation funnel missing the vocabulary while the application funnel had it; today is the same pair of funnels with the finding inverted, one layer down — from the enum to the predicate that reads it.

**Deliberately not researched, by the 10-17 check:** what share of employer outcomes are rejections. A rejection is an answer at any share, so the number could not move the requirement.
