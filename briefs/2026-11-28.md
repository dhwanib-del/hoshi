## What I found

**Simplify ships this feature, and its own help centre supplies both the right shape and the hole in it.** The Gmail connection *"can recommend a tracker status update"* — *"Choose **Approve** to apply it, **Decline** to dismiss it, or **Approve All** when you want to accept the pending suggestions."* It calls a suggestion *"a proposed change to your tracker"*, warns that *"Matching depends on the email and application information, so do not assume every inbox message will appear under a tracked job"*, and tells you to *"verify the application before approving, especially if you have several roles at the same company."* Rejection is named among the examples. ([Simplify help](https://help.simplify.jobs/articles/0236686-email-integration) · [its own comparison article](https://simplify.jobs/blog/best-job-application-trackers-2026), the vendor documenting its own product, so tagged past what it claims to do.)

Suggest-then-approve is the honest shape. **`Approve All` is the hole** — it hands the guarantee back to the matcher in exactly the case the same page warns about, several roles at one employer, which is §8.5's dedup problem arriving in a mailbox. Null results worth recording: that page says nothing about accuracy, nothing about what happens on a wrong match, and nothing about whether a suggestion can come from an individual's reply rather than an automated employer mail.

**Why this mattered more than it looked.** Rule 12 (11-20) made *every* `outcome` row a numerator member, and §10.5 says an outcome row is the only thing confirming an application landed. So the one arm nobody had defined was the one arm that would enter the thesis' own measurement as a fact. And the skew is not merely unknown — it is **opposite between two rates built on the same rows**: an inbox surfaces the rejections you never bothered to log, which is the commonest confirmation there is, so the same imported rows push **response rate up** and **advance rate down** over an unchanged denominator. Rule 10 requires each rate to state its direction and had no answer for a numerator nobody had segmented. That is why the fix is a count rather than two rates: the two regimes sit inside the top half of the fraction, not in the denominator.

**Corroboration for a decision you already made, not a new one:** Simplify's page does not describe its Gmail access as read-only, and says the same connection is used to *send messages*. B-22 already chose `gmail.compose` without `gmail.send` — the scope is the guarantee, and a bug cannot send mail it was never granted.
